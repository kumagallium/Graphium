Notes from a baking textbook and a flour mill's technical sheet on how hydration relates to crumb structure.

## Key points

- Hydration is the weight of water as a share of the flour weight. Typical ranges: 60–70% for sandwich loaves, 70–80% for hearth breads
- More water makes the dough softer and more extensible, so the gas from fermentation grows into larger cells. That is why the crumb "opens"
- Whether the dough can hold the water depends on the amount and quality of protein (gluten). Higher-protein flour absorbs more water, so at the same hydration the dough is tighter
- Rule of thumb: a 1% difference in protein shifts absorption by about 2%. 75% on an 11% flour is roughly like 78–79% on a 12.5% flour
- High-hydration dough builds gluten better through rests and folds than through kneading. An autolyse (flour and water rested first) also helps
- Push hydration too far and the gluten network cannot support the water: the dough slackens, the loaf bakes flat, and the crumb ends up tighter, not more open
