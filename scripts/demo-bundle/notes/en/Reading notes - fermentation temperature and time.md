Notes on sourdough fermentation temperature and time, from a book and from the baker who shared the starter with me.

## Key points

- Bulk fermentation is best at 24–27 °C. With 20% starter, expect the dough to double in 4–5 hours in that range
- Each 1 °C rise speeds fermentation by roughly 10–15%. At 28 °C it runs about 30% faster than at 26 °C
- Above 28 °C the acid-producing bacteria take over and the dough turns sour. Acid also weakens gluten, so an overblown dough collapses easily
- Cold fermentation at 4–6 °C for 8–16 hours nearly stops the rise, but enzymes keep working and build aroma and sweetness. Cold dough is also easier to shape
- Judge the end of fermentation by volume and the spring-back of a poke, not by the clock. Recipe times mean nothing once the temperature changes

- [[Trial 4 - fermenting at 28 C|Trial 4]] (28 °C, 4 hours) collapsed because I let the clock, not the dough, decide. At 28 °C I should have stopped at 3 hours
