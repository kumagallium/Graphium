Notes from a review of layered cobalt oxides and a solid-state synthesis textbook, on the temperature up to which Ca3Co4O9 is stable.

## Key points

- Ca3Co4O9 is a structure of rock-salt-type layers stacked with CoO2 layers. The thermoelectric performance comes mainly from the CoO2 layers
- In air, above roughly 920-930 C it decomposes into Ca3Co2O6 and CoO. Once decomposition has progressed, it is hard to reverse by cooling
- Raising the sintering temperature increases density, improves grain-to-grain contact and lowers the electrical resistivity. This holds only up to the decomposition temperature; beyond it, insulating Ca3Co2O6 mixes in and the resistivity rises
- Solid-state reactions proceed only where the powders touch. Grinding and remixing in between reduces the unreacted parts
- The raw material calcium carbonate (CaCO3) readily absorbs moisture from the air. The weighed amount is off by the water absorbed, and the Ca to Co ratio goes wrong
