Measured the offset between the setpoint and the actual temperature at the sample position in the shared box furnace (unit 2). Since sintering temperature is varied in steps of a few tens of degrees, the comparison means nothing without knowing the offset.

## How I measured

- Placed a calibrated R-type thermocouple at the sample position (center of the furnace, upper shelf)
- Held each setpoint for 1 hour and read the average of the last 10 minutes
- Ramp rate 200 C/h (same as when firing samples)

## Results

| Setpoint | Actual (center) | Offset |
|---|---|---|
| 800 C | 806 C | +6 C |
| 900 C | 909 C | +9 C |
| 920 C | 931 C | +11 C |
| 950 C | 963 C | +13 C |

- The higher the temperature, the more the actual temperature exceeds the setpoint
- The front of the shelf is 5-8 C lower than the center

## Things to watch

- Always place samples at the center. Placed at the front, the sintering conditions change by a few degrees
- The "920 C" of [[Sample 2 - sintering at 920 C|sample 2]] was actually fired at around 931 C. That is close to where decomposition starts, so the margin is smaller than it looks
