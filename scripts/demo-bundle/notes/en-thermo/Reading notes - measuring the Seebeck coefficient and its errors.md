Notes from an explainer article on thermoelectric measurement and the instrument manual, on how the Seebeck coefficient is measured and where the errors come from.

## Key points

- The Seebeck coefficient S is found by applying a small temperature difference ΔT across the sample and using the resulting voltage ΔV: S = −ΔV/ΔT
- If ΔT is measured at only one point, any offset in thermocouple placement becomes error directly. It is more reliable to vary ΔT over several points and take the slope against ΔV
- Poor contact between electrode and sample makes the voltage unstable. Polish the end faces of the sample so the contact is the same every time
- The electrical resistivity ρ is measured by the four-probe method. Any mistake in the cross-sectional area or the distance between probes becomes error in ρ directly
- The power factor PF = S²/ρ is often used to compare samples

## How I handle it in my records

- For every sample, I take ΔT at 4 points at room temperature (300 K) and get S from the slope
- Samples are cut to 2 × 2 × 10 mm and the end faces are polished. Dimensions are measured 3 times with calipers and averaged
- In [[Sample 1 - baseline (sintering at 900 C)|sample 1]], I remeasured the same sample 3 times, and the scatter in S was ±2 μV/K
