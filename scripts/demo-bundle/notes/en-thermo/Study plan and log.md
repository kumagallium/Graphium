Make polycrystalline samples of the layered cobalt oxide Ca3Co4O9 by solid-state reaction and measure the Seebeck coefficient and electrical resistivity. Fix a baseline first, then move one condition at a time: sintering temperature, then intermediate grinding, pressing pressure, element substitution, and raw material lot. This is the index; the details are in each sample's record. (This study and its numbers are fictional, made up for the demo.)

## The samples in order

1. [[Sample 1 - baseline (sintering at 900 C)|Sample 1]] — baseline. Calcination 900 C, sintering 900 C. Single phase. S = 128 μV/K, ρ = 14 mΩcm
2. [[Sample 2 - sintering at 920 C|Sample 2]] — sintering at 920 C. Still single phase, density went up and ρ went down
3. [[Sample 3 - sintering at 950 C|Sample 3]] — sintering at 950 C. A peak that looks like Ca3Co2O6 appeared and ρ went up
4. [[Sample 4 - two intermediate grindings|Sample 4]] — still 920 C, with 2 intermediate grindings. Grains became uniform and ρ went down further
5. [[Sample 5 - higher pressing pressure|Sample 5]] — pressing 100 to 200 MPa. Density went up, but cracks appeared after sintering
6. [[Sample 6 - partial replacement of Ca with Bi|Sample 6]] — 5% Bi substitution. S went up and the power factor is the highest so far
7. [[Sample 7 - repeat with a new lot of CaCO3|Sample 7]] — same conditions as sample 4, only the raw material lot changed. Impurities appeared and it did not reproduce

## What I know so far

- Up to 920 C, sintering raises density and lowers ρ, but at 950 C it decomposes and Ca3Co2O6 appears (sample 2 against sample 3)
- More intermediate grinding makes the reaction proceed more uniformly at the same temperature
- Even under the same conditions, the result changes when the state of the raw material changes (sample 4 against sample 7). The conditions are not set by temperature and time alone
